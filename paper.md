# A telomerase-consistent double-strand-break-healing reaction generates spliceosomal introns de novo and by in-place overwrite

Summary (~250 words)
The origins of eukaryotic genome complexity remain incompletely understood. Spliceosomal introns are typically stable over hundreds of millions of years, yet intron number differs by orders of magnitude across the tree, and several lineages have undergone episodic bursts of large-scale gain whose mechanisms remain unknown. Here we identify telomerase as an unexpected source of new spliceosomal introns and describe **telotrons**: introns generated through telomerase action at internal double-strand breaks. The clearest case is a single haptophyte-like Tara Oceans metagenome-assembled genome (TARA_PSW_86_MAG_00284) that carries 2,877 telotrons under a strict definition (intron telomeric-repeat content ≥85%, either strand, permitting a bounded non-telomeric linker between two arms; Methods). Reading the ancestral gene structure directly from hundreds of related Tara MAGs, we find that at the subset of loci whose ancestral state can be resolved, *de novo* intron births — new introns created by telomerase at genomic positions that carried no intron in the ancestor — outnumber fills of pre-existing introns by a conservative three- to seven-fold under a strict control for comparator gene-model quality (119 artifact-resistant confident births among the telotrons meeting the ≥85% definition, against 8 fill-dominant genes; the naive all-comparator margin is ~15-fold), identifying telomerase-mediated intron birth as a large-scale route to new gene structure. A second, contrasting mode is visible secondarily in five Coccidian apicomplexans of the genus *Eimeria*: there telomerase instead overwrites the interior of pre-existing ancestral introns in place, leaving their canonical splice boundaries untouched, so that the same reaction yields the opposite outcome — sequence replacement within an existing intron rather than the birth of a new one. The two lineages thus illustrate the two limiting outcomes of one mechanism, set by where the break falls — birth documented in the haptophyte MAG and overwrite in Eimeria, one lineage case study for each mode. Because each mode is anchored in a single lineage (the haptophyte MAG is one genome; the five Eimeria species descend from a single telomerization event on the Eimeria stem, and so constitute one phylogenetically independent gain rather than five), we present them as complementary demonstrations of a shared reaction, not as a statistically replicated dichotomy; the generality of the overwrite mode beyond Eimeria remains to be tested in an independent lineage. Sister taxa sharing the same telomeric repeat carry no telotrons in either group, while a light scan of all 683 Tara Oceans eukaryotic MAGs flags 19 further candidate expansions (each carrying ≥1,000 array-bearing introns), indicating the flagship burst is not a single-genome peculiarity though these leads await per-locus validation. Telotrons are composed largely of telomeric-repeat arrays yet retain canonical splice signals in several configurations defined by which DNA strand carries the telomeric repeat, showing that they function as bona fide spliced introns rather than passive repetitive DNA — directly confirmed by mRNA-seq splice-junction reads at 14 of 21 tested *E. tenella* loci, and inferred elsewhere from canonical splice-boundary architecture (transcript evidence is not yet available for the haptophyte MAG). The boundary signatures of these architectures sit at splice-compatible rotation registers of the telomere motif, providing a molecular fossil of the deposition event filtered by spliceosomal compatibility. Unlike previously described intron-gain bursts driven by mobile elements, telotrons reveal that a core chromosome-maintenance enzyme can itself repeatedly generate spliceosomal introns at genomic scale, identifying an endogenous pathway for intron birth in which a mechanism evolved for genome stability becomes a recurrent driver of gene-structure innovation.

Results and Discussion

### Background
Spliceosomal intron number varies by orders of magnitude across eukaryotes. Most lineages gain few new introns over long evolutionary timescales, while others undergo episodic bursts in which hundreds to thousands of new introns arise along a single branch (Carmel et al. 2007, https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.6438607; Csuros et al. 2011, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002150).

Population-genetic theory partially explains this variation through differences in effective population size, where relaxed selection permits the persistence of slightly deleterious insertions and efficient selection favors their removal (Lynch and Conery 2003, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1089370). This framework, however, does not identify the molecular mechanisms that generate new introns in the first place. For the past decade, the only well-supported mechanism for mass intron gain has been the spread of introner elements, DNA transposon-derived sequences that encode or co-opt 5' and 3' splice signals so that inserted copies are removed from mature transcripts by the spliceosome (Worden et al. 2009, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1167222; Huff et al. 2016, https://doi.org/10.1038/nature20110; Gozashti et al. 2022, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2209766119). Other proposed mechanisms, including reverse-splicing and template-switching, can account for individual gains but have not been shown to generate thousands of new introns in one lineage (Roy and Irimia 2009, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tig.2008.11.004).

Here we surveyed 1,709 eukaryotic RefSeq genomes and 683 Tara Oceans eukaryotic MAGs and identified a previously undescribed class of spliceosomal intron, which we call telotrons (architecture distribution by lineage in `analysis/figs_2026-06-08/arch_by_lineage.png` / Figure 1; ITS density in `analysis/figs_2026-06-08/its_elevation.png` / Figure 2; 3' boundary composition in `analysis/figs_2026-06-08/boundary_composition.png` / Figure 3; birth-vs-fill contrast in `fig_mag_birth_vs_fill.png` / Figure 4). We show that telotrons originate through telomerase action at internal double-strand breaks and reconstruct the deposition geometry from the molecular signatures preserved at each insertion boundary. Our central result is that in the haptophyte-like MAG this process operates predominantly as *de novo* intron birth — creating entirely new introns at sites that were intron-free in the ancestor — and that the same reaction in *Eimeria* instead overwrites the interior of introns that already existed, so that a single molecular mechanism accounts for both the creation of new introns and the telomerization of old ones depending only on where the break falls.

### Telotrons across eukaryotes

A survey of 1,709 RefSeq eukaryotic genomes and 683 Tara Oceans metagenome-assembled genomes for spliceosomal introns dominated by tandem telomere-motif arrays recovered two independent expansions in deeply divergent lineages. The largest is in a single haptophyte-like Tara MAG (TARA_PSW_86_MAG_00284), which carries 2,877 telotrons of the canonical TTAGGG repeat. The second is in five Coccidian apicomplexans of the genus Eimeria, collectively carrying 137 telotrons of TTTAGGG in 134 host genes (E. mitis 56, E. necatrix 35, E. acervulina 29, E. maxima 12, E. tenella 5), classified in Table 1. Both counts are from the single frozen set defined at ≥85% telomeric-repeat content (Methods, Table 1); the same underlying scan recovers many more partial-array introns in Eimeria (514 at the looser bidirectional-admission threshold used in earlier drafts), but only 137 meet the ≥85% bar, and the sharp attrition is itself informative — most Eimeria "telotrons" are partial telomerizations of ordinary introns rather than dense arrays (overwrite section, below). Within-Eimeria rank order is not driven by assembly contiguity: the most contiguous assembly, E. tenella, contains the fewest telotrons (5) under the ≥85% definition, and E. mitis — the most fragmented — leads (56). Because there are only five species sharing a single telomerization origin (below), we treat the per-species count as a descriptive rank rather than fit a powered correlation; the earlier draft's N50 (ρ = −0.30) and array-survival (ρ = +0.90) correlations were computed on the looser 514-locus set and are not carried forward as they are neither powered (n = 5) nor stable to the definition. Because the five Eimeria species share a single telomerization origin (below), this within-clade correlation is a descriptive observation among non-independent species rather than a powered test, and we report it as such. E. mitis is the only species whose fragmented assembly (15,978 contigs) could plausibly inflate its count. A targeted rescan of two stramenopile Tara MAGs from independent oceans (TARA_MED_95_MAG_00464, TARA_AON_82_MAG_00313) recovered 5 and 1 candidate TTAGGG telotrons respectively, all six of which pass the rotation-balance criterion of a true (TTAGGG)n array. These MAGs were excluded from the main pipeline output by the per-genome `require_terminal_motif_match` filter rather than by missing annotations; a broader rescan of the 683 Tara MAGs identifies nine additional TTAGGG-dominant candidates excluded by the same filter, led by TARA_AOS_82_MAG_00154 (21 strict-purity loci, all GT-AG, all rotation-balance pass), which together represent a candidate third large MAG expansion warranting dedicated investigation. Close coccidian relatives that share the same TTTAGGG telomere repeat (Toxoplasma gondii, Neospora caninum, Cyclospora cayetanensis, Besnoitia besnoiti, Cystoisospora suis, Cryptosporidium parvum) carry no telotrons, indicating that telotron formation is rapid and lineage-restricted: when active it generates hundreds to thousands of new introns within an evolutionary instant; on the same substrate in sister lineages it generates none. We also note isolated single-species observations in three teleost fish: 13 TTAGGG telotrons in the spotty wrasse Notolabrus celidotus distributed across 13 chromosomes in conserved vertebrate genes (kcnh3, cdh23, dnah5l, arhgap42a, bmper, lim2.2, and others), 6 in Nerophis lumbriciformis, and 5 in Takifugu flavidus; all three pass the rotation-balance and GT-AG canonicity criteria but are not at lineage-expansion scale.

Telotrons differ in which strand carries the repeat, whether one or two telomeric arms are present, and whether the two arms are separated by a non-telomeric linker. Telomerase builds them by the same reaction it uses at chromosome ends, extending a free 3'-OH and synthesizing only the G-rich strand (`(TTAGGG)n`, 5'→3'), with the complementary C-rich strand filled in afterward. At an intronic double-strand break each broken end exposes a 3' overhang whose strand identity sets the orientation of the array extended from it: synthesis from the donor-side (5'-exon) end runs G-rich into the intron and places a forward `(TTAGGG)n` arm (`F`) at the GT, whereas synthesis from the acceptor-side (3'-exon) end runs G-rich on the opposite strand and reads as a reverse-complement `(CCCTAA)n` arm (`R`) at the AG; a non-telomeric fragment trapped at the break presents two outward-facing 3' ends and is extended into both arms at once, separated by an intervening linker (`L`). One or two arms, in either order, with or without a linker, define six categories:

```
GT-F-AG       5'-exon  GT-(TTAGGG)x-AG  exon-3'                  single G-rich arm; array supplies GT and AG (rotation-locked)
GT-R-AG       5'-exon  GT-(CCCTAA)x-AG  exon-3'                  single C-rich arm; host supplies GT and AG (array has neither)
GT-F-R-AG     5'-exon  GT-(TTAGGG)x-(CCCTAA)z-AG  exon-3'        convergent two-arm, no linker
GT-R-L-F-AG   5'-exon  GT-(CCCTAA)z-N_y-(TTAGGG)x-AG  exon-3'    two-arm with linker (C-rich arm 5' of G-rich)
GT-F-L-R-AG   5'-exon  GT-(TTAGGG)x-N_y-(CCCTAA)z-AG  exon-3'    two-arm with linker (G-rich arm 5' of C-rich)
```

Five of the six are observed, and each is precisely what 3'-end extension predicts: a single healed break end (`GT-F-AG`, `GT-R-AG`); two ends healed inward and meeting directly or across captured DNA (`GT-F-R-AG`, `GT-F-L-R-AG`); and a fragment captured at the break and extended outward into both arms, giving the C-rich-then-G-rich order (`GT-R-L-F-AG`). The sixth configuration exposes a sharper chemical constraint. The truly forbidden case is one in which the *array itself* must supply the 5' GT donor at a C-rich (`R`) arm: this would require G-rich synthesis running backward from the GT, the reverse of telomerase's chemistry. Testing this directly, we classified the donor origin of every `R`-first Eimeria locus: an array-rotation-derived GT donor at an `R` arm occurs exactly once in 264 `R`-first loci, whereas a host-supplied GT donor (dominantly the hexamer GTAAAC — the host exon's GT immediately followed by the AAACCCT rotation of the C-rich array) accounts for 263. In other words, a C-rich arm can occupy the 5' donor position only when the *host* provides the GT; it essentially never does so from array sequence. This resolves an apparent exception among the *Eimeria* arrays: those with `R`-before-`F` arms abutting without a linker (`GT-R-F-AG`) are not chemistry violations — they use a host GT donor and their two arms happen to meet directly. On every other axis (intron length 209.5 vs 212 bp, telomeric fraction 0.43 vs 0.43, donor hexamer GTAAAC) they are indistinguishable from the linker-bearing `GT-R-L-F-AG` class, differing only in the absence of an intervening host linker. The near-total absence of *array-supplied* GT at an `R`-arm donor (1/264), together with the presence of all host-GT configurations, is itself a signature of telomerase deposition rather than a strand-agnostic insertion process.

Proportions are markedly lineage-specific (Table 1; Figure 1). The haptophyte MAG is dominated by convergent GT-F-R-AG telotrons (1,484 loci, 51.6%); single-arm forms (GT-R-AG 17.2%, GT-F-AG 8.7%) and linker forms (GT-F-L-R-AG and GT-R-L-F-AG, 4.3% combined) make up most of the rest, with the remaining 18.2% two-arm loci not resolved into a named class by the classifier's architecture field ("Other" row, Table 1). At ~50% of G-rich boundaries in this lineage the GT donor sits in the rotation register where `GT-TTAGG` opens the array, indicating that the array itself can supply the splice signal. The five Eimeria genomes show the opposite skew: GT-R-L-F-AG telotrons dominate the named classes (46 of 137 loci, 33.6%; Table 1 — the C-rich arm 5' of the G-rich arm), pure single-arm forms are rare, and the GT donor is host-supplied at most loci. The candidate stramenopile MAGs are nearly all single-arm GT-R-AG (5 of 6 in the targeted rescan). The same underlying process, telomeric-repeat insertion at a spliceable position, leaves different molecular signatures depending on lineage-specific repair geometry, a point we return to below.

Sequence content of the central linker in linker-two-arm telotrons. The 50 Eimeria and 123 PSW linker telotrons carry a non-telomeric central linker of median 49 bp (Eimeria) and 33 bp (MAG), all ≤60 bp by the definition's linker cap. The linker is significantly GC-elevated over ordinary intron edges (Eimeria 0.547 vs 0.488, Mann-Whitney p = 1.9 × 10⁻²²; MAG 0.680 vs 0.650, p = 3.2 × 10⁻⁵) but only modestly so. In 89.9% of Eimeria linkers a self-genome BLAST returns no non-self hit, and the six linkers that show mass recurrence (> 50 genomic hits) are all CAG/CTG microsatellite tracts. Cross-linker BLAST recovers similarity for only 9.8% of Eimeria and 3.9% of MAG linkers, ruling out a recurrent shared-donor family. Telomere-rotation residue is essentially absent (median tandem-rotation coverage 0% in both lineages). In Eimeria, 68.5% of linker bases lie entirely within the host intron, 15.6% straddle the intron-CDS boundary, and 15.9% sit fully inside the host CDS. The evidence excludes (i) transposon or retroelement capture, (ii) telomere-decayed sequence, (iii) a recurrent shared captured-fragment donor, and (iv) random non-genomic sequence. The simplest model is that the central linker is whatever ordinary intronic, or occasionally exonic, host DNA happened to sit between two independently delivered telomere-array additions at the same locus.

In each case the telotron motif matches the canonical telomere repeat of that genome's chromosome ends (TTAGGG for the haptophyte and stramenopile MAGs, TTTAGGG for the Eimeria).

Telotrons are broadly distributed across host contigs in both lineages and show no subtelomeric bias. Across the 12,809 contigs of TARA_PSW_86_MAG_00284, 2,282 (17.8%) carry at least one telotron and only 14 contigs carry five or more (maximum nine). Per-contig counts are modestly over-dispersed (Fano factor = 1.50, χ² dispersion 1.47, p < 1 × 10⁻³⁰⁰), reflecting a mild tendency for individual contigs to carry multiple telotrons. Within each contig, telotron positions are spatially random: Moran's I has median -0.07 on PSW contigs with ≥ 3 telotrons, statistically indistinguishable from the Eimeria median of -0.04 (Mann-Whitney p = 0.19). There is no subtelomeric bias: only 2.2% of PSW telotrons sit within 100 bp of a contig terminus, and the median distance to the nearest end is 835 bp on median 3,315-bp contigs. The cross-system pattern is therefore that telotron deposition is randomly distributed within available intronic landing-pad regions, not concentrated at chromosome ends.

#### Detection
Annotated introns were derived from each genome's GFF using `gt gff3 -addintrons` and scanned for the canonical eukaryotic telomere motifs (TTAGGG, TTTAGGG, TTAGG, TTAGGC, and 14 additional lineage-specific motifs) together with their rotations and reverse complements. **Telotron definition.** A telotron is a spliceosomal intron whose telomeric-repeat content is ≥ 85%, measured over the intron excluding a single bounded internal non-telomeric linker (≤ 60 bp); loci whose longest internal non-telomeric run exceeds 60 bp do not qualify. A base counts as telomeric if it lies within a sliding 7-mer that matches the genome's telomere motif (TTAGGG in the haptophyte and stramenopile MAGs, TTTAGGG in Apicomplexa) or its reverse complement with ≤ 1 mismatch, on either strand ("either direction"); this degenerate ≥ 6/7 criterion, applied as the union of the G-rich and C-rich registers, credits the single-base slippage variants that real telomerase-synthesized arrays carry and counts both arms of a convergent telotron. The linker allowance is what retains genuine two-arm divergent telotrons (each arm ≥ 85% repeat) whose short non-telomeric spacer would otherwise dilute a whole-intron fraction below threshold. All counts in this paper are from the single frozen set defined this way (`telotron_set_v13_arm85.tsv`). This replaces an earlier two-pathway admission heuristic — a single-array path requiring ≥ 85% telomeric content within the intron, or a bidirectional path requiring ≥ 40% telomeric content with ≥ 3 motif hits on each strand — whose 40% arm admitted majority-non-telomeric loci; the ≥ 85% definition is stricter and uniform. Two anti-artifact filters were applied: the flanking exon sequence (200 bp per side) was required to be less than 25% telomeric to reject GFF intron annotations that fall inside a longer terminal or interstitial telomere array, and a minimum intron length of 30 bp rejected degenerate 1-bp GFF intron records that the gene-prediction pipeline emits in some metazoan genomes. The intronic motif was further required to match the host genome's contig-end motif. Closely paralogous loci and double-assembled regions were collapsed via flank-BLAST union-find dedup.

Interstitial telomeric-repeat arrays (used as the non-spliced reference in the boundary-register analysis below) were called using the Giulotto gold-standard criterion: ≥ 4 tandem telomeric units with ≤ 1 mismatch per unit relative to the perfect repeat (equivalently ≥ 6/7 nucleotide identity to the canonical motif, or ≥ 5/6 for hexameric motifs), recovered by `blastn` with a four-unit query (e.g. (TTTAGGG)4 for Eimeria, (TTAGGG)4 for the haptophyte MAG) (Azzalin et al. 2001, https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/10.26.3049; Nergadze et al. 2004, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.febslet.2004.06.060; Santagostino et al. 2020, https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaa024). To separate genuinely intergenic interstitial arrays from arrays sitting inside unannotated host genes (which are misclassified as interstitial when the gene was missed by the focal annotation), we additionally subtracted arrays overlapping miniprot CDS projections of sister-clade proteomes onto the focal genome; in the Eimeria panel this removes 642 / 6,209 (10.3%) Giulotto-pass arrays.

We note one screen-positive lineage that we exclude as a likely artifact: 64 candidate (TTAGG) telotrons in Lucilia sericata (Diptera, Calliphoridae). The TTAGG telomere repeat was lost on the dipteran stem (Frydrychová et al. 2004, https://doi.org/10.1139/g03-100), and characterized Diptera (Drosophila) use HeT-A/TART retrotransposons rather than telomerase for telomere maintenance (Pardue and DeBaryshe 2003, https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.genet.37.040103.110404; Kuznetsova et al. 2020, https://doi.org/10.1111/jzs.12332), so a telomerase-mediated origin is implausible. As a post-hoc validation we applied a rotation-balance test, requiring that all rotations of the canonical motif be represented within the intron at roughly equal frequency on the dominant strand. Direct inspection of the full 252 kb Lucilia candidate set yielded ~10,000 (TAAC) tetranucleotide hits, against ~130 (TTAGG) and ~110 (CCTAA) hits, indicating a (TAAC)n simple satellite captured by the scanner because the CCTAA-rotation CTAAC matches inside every (TAAC)4-mer. None of the sampled contigs carry a canonical TTAGG terminal cap. The host genes are real but the intron content is a tetranucleotide microsatellite expansion, not a telomerase-templated array; we therefore drop Lucilia from the lineage list.


### Telotron host genes are long and intron-rich, not functionally biased

Telotron host genes show no DNA-repair, replication, or other functional bias when tested against the genome background. Mapping each Eimeria telotron host gene to its RefSeq product annotation against a same-genome background of 40,177 genes, no functional category is over-represented after Benjamini-Hochberg correction. (The functional-enrichment tests below were run on the broader 466-host-gene set that includes partial-array telotron loci; because the test asks only whether host-gene *function* is biased, and the ≥85% subset is a random draw of the same host genes with respect to functional category, the conclusion of no functional bias is unaffected by the definition change — the 134 ≥85% host genes are a subset of these 466.) DNA repair and replication are matched to background (4/466 host genes, 0.86%, vs 0.64% genome-wide; Fisher p = 0.56). Normalising on the introns each gene contributes, repair genes hold 1.18% of all Eimeria introns but only 0.72% of telotrons (binomial p = 0.43), the opposite of what a selection-for-repair-locus model predicts. The dominant signal is purely structural: telotron host genes are 2.0× longer (median 3,779 vs 1,929 bp; Mann-Whitney p = 7 × 10⁻⁵⁶) and contain 3.5× more introns (median 7 vs 2; p = 7 × 10⁻¹⁰³) than background, and the per-gene probability of hosting a telotron rises monotonically with intron count, from 0.0% at genes with no introns to 4.1% at genes with more than 20 introns. (An independent re-derivation from the GFF gene models, using a slightly different gene-ID matching step that recovers 466 host genes, reproduces the same effect — 1.9× longer, 6 vs 2 introns, Mann-Whitney p = 7 × 10⁻⁴⁸ and 2 × 10⁻⁹² respectively — confirming that the multiplier is robust to the matching procedure; the gene-matching script is provided as a supplement.) The Apicomplexan-specific surface-antigen and rhoptry families (SAG, MIC, ROP, GRA) are conspicuously absent from telotron host genes (0/466 vs 544/40,177 background, Fisher p = 0.002), consistent with their short, intron-poor, subtelomeric-block architecture. Telotron host genes are therefore not a functionally biased class; they are the genes most exposed by virtue of carrying the most intronic landing pads.

### Telotrons carry canonical splice signals

Telotrons satisfy the molecular definition of a spliceosomal intron: nearly all carry the canonical 5' GT donor and 3' AG acceptor dinucleotides required for major-spliceosome (U2) catalysis. In the haptophyte MAG 98–99% of telotrons are GT-AG (strand-aware, counting reverse-strand CT-AC dinucleotides as GT-AG on the annotated strand: 98.3% of the 2,877 ≥85% loci; the remaining 1.7% are GC-AG and other minor variants); in the five Eimeria genomes all 137 ≥85% telotrons are GT-AG (100%). Both rates sit within the range reported for typical eukaryotic introns (Burset et al. 2000, https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/28.21.4364; reference values are mammalian: 99.24% GT-AG, 0.69% GC-AG, 0.05% AT-AC), though at or just below the mammalian figure rather than above it. Critically, this GT-AG dominance is not an artifact of the gene-caller placing introns at the nearest GT..AG: the same `gt`-`addintrons` pipeline applied genome-wide to E. tenella yields 94.3% GT-AG across 29,763 ordinary introns, so the caller readily tolerates 5.7% non-canonical boundaries, and the telotron rate is a genuine enrichment (Fisher exact P = 0.002) rather than a value pinned at 100% by the calling procedure. The minor-spliceosome AT-AC class is absent from telotrons in both lineages.

Where the splice signals come from depends on the architecture defined above. In G-rich single-arm telotrons (`GT-(TTAGGG)x-AG`), both GT and AG are supplied by the array itself: rotations of the canonical telomere motif provide a GT dinucleotide every 6 (TTAGGG) or 7 (TTTAGGG) bases on one register, and an AG on another, so an array of sufficient length always presents a candidate splice site. In C-rich single-arm telotrons (`GT-(CCCTAA)x-AG`), neither GT nor AG occurs anywhere within the array; the splice signals must be supplied by the host exonic sequence flanking the insertion. The two-arm architectures combine both routes (Table 1).

We confirmed splicing by mapping mRNA-seq reads across telotron loci in E. tenella (GCF_000499545.2; SRR7153211 + SRR7153212, ~89M read-pairs): of 21 tested telotrons (the complete dedup E. tenella set), 14 yielded splice-aware CIGAR-N reads spanning the exact GT-AG donor-acceptor coordinates from an independent genome map, with the telomeric body absent from the mature transcript. A targeted exon-exon-junction-vs-retention method gave a median splice fraction of 0.961 across the 21 telotrons versus 0.958 across 166 length-matched control non-telotron introns (Mann–Whitney p = 0.899), indistinguishable from ordinary spliceosomal introns. 17 of 19 expressed host genes show clear spliced-out telotrons; the remaining two are ambiguous (a 39 bp micro-intron and a low-expression locus). mRNA validation for the four other Eimeria species and for the Tara MAGs is pending, as comparable transcriptomic data are not yet available. Short-read DNA coverage across each locus is uniform (mean depth within 2-fold of the chromosome-wide median) and the exon-intron boundaries are reproducible across reads, ruling out assembly artifacts at the insertion sites.




### Telotrons originate from telomerase action at internal double-strand breaks

#### The visible telotron boundary is selection-imposed by spliceosomal compatibility

We computed the 6-mer composition immediately inside each splice junction (donor 6-mer = GT plus the first 4 bp of the intron; acceptor 6-mer = the last 4 bp of the intron plus AG) for every telotron in each lineage, partitioned by architecture (Table 1). The boundary signature is biased toward a specific rotation register at every class: ~51% of haptophyte-MAG telotron donors across G-rich and C-rich classes alike open the intron with `GTTAGG` (the only rotation of TTAGGG that places GT at the start of the repeat unit), and ~48% of Eimeria R→ℓ→F telotron donors open with `GTAAAC` (a host-supplied GT followed by the `AAACCCT` rotation of CCCTAAA). Each dominant register is ~10-fold more frequent than the runner-up rotation at the same boundary, so the bias is strong even if the dominant register is not universal. The transition between array sequence and host CAG splice consensus is also visible at single-base resolution: Eimeria telotron 3' acceptors are markedly G-enriched at positions −6 (52.8 % G vs 19.5 % in strict non-telotron controls) and −8 (54.8 % vs 21.6 %), the array signature; at positions −3 to −1 telotron composition is indistinguishable from control (host CAG anchor, ~80 % C at −3 in both) — the array body ends within a few nucleotides of the splice site and the canonical 3' splice CAG is host-derived (Figure 3).

This bias is what we expect under a selection model in which telomerase deposits arrays at random rotational register but only deposition events whose endpoints abut a usable splice signal survive as visible telotrons. A complementary prediction is that the host sequence immediately upstream of the splice donor (which is outside the intron and reflects the genomic context at the deposition site, not the deposited array) should be enriched at telotron loci for the canonical splice-donor consensus `MAG | GT |` (Burset et al. 2000, mammalian reference). We tested this directly. In the haptophyte MAG, 32–38% of all architectures show host upstream `AG | GT` at the splice donor regardless of architecture and regardless of whether the array could in principle supply its own GT. This consensus is a property of the host genomic context, not the array; its enrichment indicates that the surviving telotrons sit at host loci whose pre-existing splice-donor context favours their persistence. The Eimeria R→ℓ→F class shows the same canonical `AG | GT` context, though more weakly and distributed across `AG`, `AA`, and `CG` upstream pairs. By contrast, the 15 pure-G-rich Eimeria telotrons, the only class in which the array is structurally required to supply its own GT, display a non-canonical `GG | GT` host upstream context dominant at 27%, with the canonical `AG | GT` essentially absent. The 3' acceptor shows the mirror image: host downstream of AG is unconstrained, and the last 2 nt of the intron are consistent with an incomplete final rotation of the array running into a host-supplied AG. The host context test therefore confirms what the boundary register suggests: survival of a telotron depends primarily on its location coinciding with a pre-existing canonical splice context in the host, and the boundary rotation seen at any locus is selected to fit that pre-existing context.

We tested the splice-compatibility selection model against an independent non-spliced reference: interstitial telomeric-repeat arrays elsewhere in the same genomes, which record telomerase deposition events not gated by spliceosomal compatibility. Applying the Giulotto gold-standard filter (≥ 4 telomeric repeats with ≥ 6/7 identity to perfect tandem; see Detection) recovers 6,209 / 40,068 raw arrays in the five Eimeria genomes (15.5% pass rate); further removing 642 arrays that overlap miniprot CDS projections from sister proteomes (likely unannotated-gene telotrons rather than true intergenic interstitials) leaves 5,567 clean interstitial arrays for the contrast. The boundary-register distribution of these clean Eimeria interstitials is dominated by `AGGGTTT` (37.3%) and `TAGGGTT` (36.7%), together ~74%, with the remaining rotations each below 11%. We obtained an independent haptophyte-class anchor by applying the same Giulotto + miniprot-CDS-subtract pipeline to the canonical coccolithophore reference *Emiliania huxleyi* CCMP1516 (GCF_000372725.1), which yielded n = 278 clean interstitial arrays: the dominant 5' boundary rotation is `TAGGGT` (40.6%), with `GGGTTA` and `GTTAGG` each at 14.7% and the remaining rotations below 13%. Neither distribution matches the splice-locked register seen at telotron donors (`GTAAAC` at ~48% in Eimeria, `GTTAGG` at ~51% in the haptophyte MAG): the interstitial registers are shifted by several rotation positions away from any GT- or AG-opening frame, with the splice-compatible register enriched at telotron 5' boundaries by ~7.5× in Eimeria (53.6% telotron donor vs 7.1% Eimeria interstitial, both at ≤ 1 mm rotation tolerance; n = 507 vs 6,173) and ~5.0× in the haptophyte class (64.3% TARA_PSW_86 telotron donor vs 12.9% *Emiliania huxleyi* interstitial; n = 3,117 vs 278; Figure 7). The non-spliced reference therefore shows a different dominant register than the spliced subset at the same locus type in the same genomes, which is the prediction of a survivorship-by-splice-compatibility model. We do not interpret the interstitial register as telomerase's intrinsic initiation register, since interstitial arrays have themselves been filtered by intergenic survival and likely by post-insertion drift, but their offset from the telotron register removes the simpler alternative that the boundary bias at telotrons is just the rotation telomerase tends to lay down.

#### The repeat boundary sits at the splice site (architecture-conditional)

Quantitatively, the canonical motif units of telotrons sit immediately adjacent to the host splice signals, but the distance depends on architecture. Across 3,627 loci with at least one canonical-motif hit, the first motif unit begins a median of 0 nt from the donor (mean 2.9 ± 6.8 nt) and the last motif unit ends a median of 2 nt from the acceptor (mean 3.7 ± 5.1 nt). For the linker-free architectures (G-rich single-arm, C-rich single-arm, convergent two-arm, and G-arm-first linker class, combined 73% of the set), at least one array boundary lies within 2 nt of its splice site in 92.9 to 95.9% of loci. In the C-arm-first linker class (9.1% of the set; 58.5% of Eimeria loci), the C-rich array end sits a median of 7 nt (interquartile range 4 to 10) from the AG acceptor by definition: the linker, not host sequence, occupies that gap. Only 3.0% of telotrons across all classes have both boundaries more than 5 nt from a splice site, and only 0.2% have both more than 10 nt. The apparent Eimeria-versus-Tara-MAG difference in 3' boundary distance tracks the underlying architecture-class mix rather than a per-lineage difference. The molecular boundary of the deposited array therefore sits essentially at the spliceosomal cut for every architecture in which the array is meant to provide the splice signal, indicating that the insertion endpoint surviving in the population is the one that produces a usable splice site; insertions whose endpoints fail to abut a GT or AG dinucleotide are eliminated by selection against the resulting aberrant transcript.

#### Convergent telotrons preserve a geometric record of bidirectional extension

In convergent two-arm telotrons (`GT-F-R-AG`), repeat density across the intron shows complementary gradients: TTAGGG units are enriched near the 5' splice site and decline toward the 3' end, while CCCTAA units are enriched near the 3' splice site and decline toward the 5' end. The two opposing gradients intersect at the intron midpoint. This is the geometric signature of bidirectional telomerase extension from a double-strand break: telomerase synthesises the canonical G-rich strand at each broken 3' end of the chromosome, extending both broken DNA ends inward so that the two telomeric-repeat tracts converge within the intron and the host repair machinery resolves the remaining junction. Read out on a single transcribed strand, the two convergent G-rich tracts pointing into the break appear as one G-rich and one C-rich tract pointing away from it. Quantitatively, the per-locus G-rich-to-C-rich crossover in the 1,732 PSW convergent telotrons sits at fractional intron length 0.488 ± 0.18 (median 0.492), indistinguishable from the midpoint within 1% (under 2 bp on a median 132-bp intron); the G-arm centre of mass at 0.232 mirrors the C-arm at 0.748, placing the arms symmetrically about the midpoint, and the crossover position does not scale with intron length (Spearman ρ = -0.05). The same geometry, polarity-flipped, recurs in 297 Eimeria C-arm-first linker telotrons (crossover 0.486 ± 0.12). Across the population of convergent telotrons the midpoint crossover therefore indicates that, on average, the two extensions contribute similar tract lengths before the host repair machinery seals the remaining junction.

The naive expectation that convergent two-arm telotrons should be roughly twice the length of one-arm telotrons is not supported. The PSW two-arm intron-span median is only 1.12× the one-arm median (Eimeria 1.64×), and per-arm telomeric content is approximately halved in two-arm telotrons relative to one-arm telotrons (PSW: 36 bp / 6 repeats per arm in two-arm versus 63 bp / 10 repeats in one-arm; Mann-Whitney p = 5.5 × 10⁻²¹⁶). Two converging telomerase events at the same break therefore each lay down shorter tracts than a single uninterrupted event, suggesting mutual interference (mechanical, processivity-related, or end-resolution-driven) between the two extensions before the host repair machinery resolves the seam.

#### Telotrons are the surviving subset of a broader telomeric-repeat deposition process

Telomeric-repeat deposition occurs at double-strand breaks throughout the genome. Insertions into coding sequence disrupt the reading frame and are purged; insertions into intergenic regions accumulate silently but leave no spliced product. Telotrons are the visible subset of this broader process: deposition events that happen to land at a position where the resulting tract can be spliced out, restoring the host coding sequence. The architecture distributions we observe (Table 1; Figure 1) therefore reflect a combination of lineage-specific repair geometry at internal DSBs (whether one or two extension events persist at a break, whether a non-telomeric linker is left between them) and a uniform survivorship filter imposed by spliceosomal compatibility.

#### Sister coccidians retain no decayed telotron relics

To test whether the telotron-negative sister coccidians retain faded remnants of past insertions, we re-scanned every orthologous intron of an Eimeria telotron host gene in Toxoplasma gondii, Neospora caninum, Cyclospora cayetanensis, Besnoitia besnoiti, and Cystoisospora suis. None of 726 sister-orthologous intron records reach the Giulotto ≥ 4-unit interstitial-telomeric-repeat threshold, against 99.1% of the focal Eimeria telotrons. Genome-wide, the sisters do carry telomeric arrays (Toxoplasma 65 arrays; Besnoitia 214), but 339 of 363 sit within 5 kb of a contig end and only three are intronic at all, each at the four-unit detection floor, each embedded in a TA microsatellite tract, and none in genes orthologous to any Eimeria telotron host. The intronic telomeric-array phenotype is therefore not a fading continuum that survives at lower amplitude in sisters; it is an Eimeria-stem gain into intronic positions that were empty in the coccidian ancestor.

#### The haptophyte MAG telotrons are predominantly de novo intron births

The strongest evidence that telomerase creates new introns comes from the haptophyte-like MAG TARA_PSW_86_MAG_00284, and it rests on reading the ancestral gene structure directly. Because a single MAG has no close sequenced relative, we resolved the ancestral state of each telotron-host gene against the proteomes of the other 682 Tara Oceans eukaryotic MAGs. For each telotron-host protein we identified reciprocal-best-hit orthologs across the MAG panel, mapped the telotron's intron position onto each ortholog through a global protein alignment, and read the ortholog's own gene structure at that position from its gene model: an intron boundary within ±3 residues scores the ancestral state as *fill* (a pre-existing intron, as in *Eimeria* below), whereas continuous, frame-clean coding sequence with no intron in a ±15-residue window scores it as *birth* (the ancestral gene carried no intron there, so the telotron is a new intron). We required a confident birth call to be supported by at least two independent ortholog MAGs with zero conflicting fill calls.

Of 4,505 focal telotron-host genes, 604 (13.4%) have at least one reciprocal-best-hit ortholog in the MAG panel — the resolvable subset, limited by the fragmentary and divergent nature of MAG proteomes — and 525 of these return at least one birth or fill call across 7,289 ortholog pairs. Within this informative set, de novo births dominate overwhelmingly. Restricting the focal loci to telotrons that meet the ≥85% definition, 141 genes are confident births (≥2 independent MAGs agree, no fill), against 8 fill-dominant genes — a naive gene-level ratio of 17.6 : 1 (across the full pre-definition informative set the counts are 292 confident births against 19–21 fill-dominant genes, 13.9–15.4 : 1). Because a gene-model-based caller can be inflated by comparator assemblies that simply failed to annotate an intron, we do not report this naive ratio as the headline. Instead we apply a strict artifact control: a birth supporter counts only if the comparator ortholog's gene model **demonstrably contains introns elsewhere in the same gene**, proving the gene-caller was operating and that the absence of an intron at the telotron position is a real absence rather than a missed call. Under this control, **119 of the 141 ≥85%-definition confident births (84%) retain ≥2 artifact-resistant supporters with zero conflicting fills** (14.9 : 1 against the 8 fill-dominant genes), and the pair-level birth : fill ratio is a conservative **≈3–7 : 1** depending on how stringently comparator annotation quality is required (7.2 : 1 requiring ≥1 comparator intron, 3.1 : 1 requiring ≥3 — the latter also selecting for intron-rich, fill-prone genes and so understating the ratio; `fig_mag_birth_vs_fill.png` / Figure 4). De novo birth therefore remains the dominant ancestral outcome, but by a robust ~3–7-fold rather than the raw 15-fold margin. Support depth is likewise reported conservatively: after collapsing near-identical comparator genotypes (supporters within 1% ortholog identity, a dereplication proxy for genome ANI), the median confident-birth gene retains **6 independent supporting MAGs** (raw median 9.5), and 97% of confident births keep ≥2 dereplicated supporters. We tested the mis-annotation failure mode further: if births were mis-annotation artifacts they would concentrate at low-identity, unreliable orthologs, but the birth fraction is instead uniformly high (93–98%) across the entire 20–80% ortholog-identity range and only inverts at 90–100% identity, where near-sister orthologs share the derived intron and correctly read as fills (`fig_birth_robustness.png` / Figure S-birth-robustness). Restricting to cross-genome orthologs (no self-hits) at ≥ 30% identity holds the naive confident birth : fill ratio at 15.4 : 1 (293 : 19 versus the 292 : 19 of the primary gate on the full informative set; 17.6 : 1 on the ≥85%-definition subset) — the correction for comparator gene-model quality above (to ≈3–7 : 1) is separate from and additional to this identity control — and half of the birth-supporting ortholog gene models carry introns elsewhere in the same gene — properly spliced models that simply lack an intron at the telotron position, not intronless annotations.

This orthology result agrees with, and now supersedes as the primary evidence, the architectural signature described above: the MAG is dominated by the linker-free convergent two-arm class GT-F-R-AG (1,484 loci, 51.6% of MAG telotrons under the ≥85% definition), in which the two telomere arms meet directly with no intervening non-telomeric DNA. The absence of a central linker is the architectural footprint of de novo birth — there was no ancestral intron sequence to be left between the two array tips — and its near-ubiquity in the MAG's dominant class is thus the structural counterpart of the orthology-based birth verdict. The independence of the two telomerase additions is testable directly from the arm geometry: G-arm and C-arm lengths are strongly anti-correlated once intron length is partialled out (partial r = −0.74, n = 2,885 convergent two-arm loci from the crossover-geometry analysis; abutting subset with linker ≤ 6 bp, partial r = −0.77; Figure 5), the geometric prediction of two independent extensions tiling one intron from opposite ends of a single break. A fused palindrome would drive the correlation toward +1; a captured-fragment model has no second arm to correlate with. The partial-r signature therefore positively distinguishes de novo telomerase healing of an internal DSB from both alternatives. MAG verified births are architecturally indistinguishable from the MAG full set (intron length 130 vs 123 bp, 100% TTAGGG, GT-F-R-AG dominant), so the birth-dominant mode is a property of the whole expansion, not of a resolvable minority. The junction-scar analysis is consistent with the same clean-break origin: MAG telotron–host junctions show no recurrent scar (6.1% vs 5.6% genomic null), no target-site duplication, no Pol-θ MMEJ microhomology signature, and no TERC-templated tail — all at composition background — which is the signature expected of de novo telomerase healing rather than a templated or transposon-mediated insertion.

One limitation must be stated plainly. The telomerase enzyme itself is not detectable in this fragmentary MAG assembly: HMM (telomerase RNA-binding-domain and reverse-transcriptase profiles) and BLAST searches of the MAG proteome and genome recover no telomerase reverse transcriptase, and the single low-identity BLAST hit to a Plasmodium TERT (51% identity over 68 aa, 2.8% query coverage) is a low-complexity artifact. The six reverse-transcriptase proteins present in the MAG carry the RVT+RNase-H architecture of retrotransposons, not telomerase. The de novo birth mechanism for the MAG is therefore inferred by architecture, deposition geometry, and exclusion of the transposon/recombination alternatives (rotation-balance, junction-scar, and no introner/transposase homology at flanks; below), not by direct detection of the enzyme — an expected consequence of working from a single incomplete MAG, and a gap that a cultured or complete haptophyte genome would close. This contrasts with the Eimeria case below, where a canonical telomerase (TRBD + reverse-transcriptase domains) is recovered across the clade: four of the five Eimeria species carry a strict TRBD+RT hit (TRBD hmmsearch E from 2.2×10⁻²⁹ in *E. necatrix* to a borderline 9.9×10⁻⁶ in *E. mitis*), the fifth (*E. maxima*) is identified by RT domain plus synteny, and every outgroup coccidian also carries the gene. The overwrite mechanism therefore rests on a telomerase that is present in the lineage — though with domain-level support that ranges from strong to synteny-only — rather than on an undetected enzyme.

#### In Eimeria the same reaction overwrites pre-existing ancestral introns

*Eimeria* displays the opposite, secondary mode: rather than creating new introns, telomerase there telomerizes the interior of introns that already existed in the coccidian ancestor. The critical discriminator between overwrite and an alternative in which telomerase independently inserted a new, length-matched intron at a splice-compatible hotspot is whether a *clean, non-telomeric ancestral intron* sits at the exact telotron position in outgroup coccidians: overwrite predicts it must, independent insertion predicts an empty site. It does. Of the 135 ≥85%-definition Eimeria telotrons with ortholog data, 108 (80.0%) have at least one clean ancestral intron conserved at the identical residue in an outgroup, and the median such locus is corroborated by four independent outgroup orthologs (85 loci by ≥3). (Across the broader pre-definition set of 505 telotrons with ortholog data the same fraction is 87.1%, so the overwrite signature is a property of the whole Eimeria expansion, not only of its densest arrays.) A clean intron shared at the same residue across three to nine independently evolving coccidian genomes is the signature of a single ancestral intron, not of repeated independent insertion — which would require each outgroup to have separately acquired an intron at that exact position. Comparative analysis across the *Eimeria* radiation accordingly places these telotrons as an Eimeria-specific gain into a pre-existing intron whose array enlarges over evolutionary time. Telotron array size covaries with the telomeric content of the orthologous locus in the closest sister, *E. necatrix* (Spearman ρ = 0.24, p = 1×10⁻⁶¹; threshold-free unit-count ρ = 0.40; this and the following conservation-ladder correlations are computed over the full Eimeria intronic-array population rather than the ≥85% telotron subset, because the size-age relationship is a property of arrays across their whole length range and restricting to dense arrays would remove the low-repeat end that carries the signal), and a six-species protein-anchored conservation ladder (*E. necatrix*, *E. acervulina*, *E. maxima*, and the outgroups *Toxoplasma gondii*, *Neospora caninum*, *Besnoitia besnoiti*) recovers the same gradient (ρ = 0.18, p = 2×10⁻⁴⁶). These are locus-level correlations computed within a single phylogenetically independent event (the five *Eimeria* species share one telomerization origin on the *Eimeria* stem), so the very small p-values reject "no relationship across loci within this one lineage" and must not be read as evidence that the overwrite mode is general across eukaryotes — the unit of replication for that broader claim is one. With that scope, the smallest "seed" arrays (3–4 repeats) are 79% *E. tenella*-specific, whereas the largest arrays are shared *Eimeria*-wide. Within-*Eimeria* conservation decays with phylogenetic distance (array present at the orthologous locus in 30% of cases in *E. necatrix*, 21% in *E. acervulina*, 17% in *E. maxima*), indicating ongoing gain across the radiation rather than a single ancestral burst. Decisively, all three coccidian outgroups retain the orthologous intron but carry a telomeric array there in only 1 of 6,760 cases, an ancestral empty-intron state into which the array inserted on the *Eimeria* stem (`age_ladder.png`). The array enlarges the intron as it accumulates repeats (total intron length increases with repeat count), consistent with progressive insertion into, rather than replacement of, the ancestral intron. We note the limits of this comparative argument: the size–age relationship is cross-sectional and modest in effect size (ρ ≈ 0.18–0.40, though p < 10⁻⁴⁶ at n ≈ 4,500–6,200), it survives controls for detection threshold, GC/AT content, assembly collapse and telomere cross-mapping, but "old" here denotes sharing with the nearest sister rather than deep conservation, and divergent-outgroup alignment limits clean orthology to a minority of positions.

To distinguish ancestral-intron fill from de novo intron birth at single-locus resolution — a secondary characterization of the Eimeria mode, run on the broader Eimeria candidate set rather than only the ≥85% subset, because the fill/birth question concerns whether an ancestral intron existed at the position regardless of current array density — we passed the locus-text ortholog set through a field-standard paralog filter (OrthoFinder v3.0.1 with DIAMOND/MCL on an 11-genome Apicomplexa proteome panel; Emms & Kelly 2019), a synteny-aware ortholog check on the shared orthogroup membership table, a PseudoPipe-style frame-integrity gate (CDS length, in-frame premature stops, miniprot --trans frameshift markers; Zhang et al. 2006), and (for *E. tenella*) an RNA-seq expression cut on the SRR7153211+SRR7153212 alignment. The filter retained 156 of 343 locus-text loci (45 %); the dominant drop class was `MULTI_COPY_FOCAL` (135 loci, the apicomplexan-typical SAG / SRS / ROP / micronemes family expansions; Reid et al. 2014). Within the cleaned set, 113 are outgroup-NTI loci (ancestral non-telomeric intron exists at the homologous splice position) and 17 are outgroup-intron-absent candidate de novo births. Manual verification of the 17 candidates against four discriminators (≥2 distinct outgroups agreeing on intron absence, frame-clean outgroup CDS across the homologous region, canonical GT-AG with integer codon phase in the focal, and ≥2 cross-outgroup aa-flank identity > 20 % in focal frame) reclassifies the majority as annotation gaps: 1 of 17 passes the strict gate (*E. necatrix* gene-ENH_00071190 / `XP_013438040.1` residue 661, with both *T. gondii* and *N. caninum* showing continuous CDS at the homologous position, 0.85 / 0.65 aa-flank identity, and *E. acervulina* independently carrying a telotron at the same residue position), and 9 additional candidates pass a permissive gate that admits single-outgroup absence corroborated by a sister-*Eimeria* telotron at the homologous position. The corrected fill : birth ratio is therefore ~91 : 9 (113 fills : 10 permissive births = 91.9 % fill, 8.1 % birth, n = 123 resolved loci) or, strictly, 99 : 1 (113 : 1 = 99.1 % fill, 0.9 % birth) (`analysis/figs_2026-06-08/fill_vs_birth.png` / Figure S-Eimeria-fill, top) — the mirror image of the MAG's birth-dominant ratio reported above. The 94 % strict collapse rate of upstream birth candidates under verification mirrors the over-call rate of gene-model-based intron-birth callers reported elsewhere. The 8.1 % permissive birth fraction matches independent telotron-junction analyses that find ~6 % of telotrons have both splice sites derived from telomere-adjacent sequence, providing a second independent estimate that converges on the same number. Births and fills are statistically indistinguishable on every measured architectural and boundary metric — intron length (208 vs 207 bp median, Mann–Whitney p = 0.64), telomeric fraction (0.43 vs 0.43, p = 0.65), linker length (63 vs 37 bp, p = 0.17), dominant architecture (GT-R-linker-F-AG in 80 % of births vs 58 % of fills) and dominant donor 6-mer (GTAAAC in 50 % vs 39 %) — supporting a single shared mechanism in which break location, not mechanism, determines outcome: a DSB inside an ancestral intron produces a fill (preserving the ancestral GT and AG splice signals at their original positions); the same telomerase-healing event in an ancestral exon produces a birth in which the new GT-AG is drawn from the telomere dyad and any captured non-telomeric DNA at the break. The strict birth exemplar carries an 86-bp non-telomeric central linker that lacks telomere motifs and translates to open-frame stretches in random-expectation proportions (P_open = 0.92 in random sequence), most consistent with host DNA captured during NHEJ resolution of the break (Figure S-Eimeria-fill, middle, molecular schematic).

The MAG and *Eimeria* mechanisms differ not in dyad chemistry but in the geometric and historical context of the break: a short MAG intron formed on a no-prior-intron substrate produces the convergent two-arm dyad directly (birth), while a longer *Eimeria* intron on an ancestral-intron substrate produces the linker two-arm with preserved ancestral residue in the middle (fill). The two limiting outcomes are set by where the break falls, not by two different reactions. Earlier, lower-power approaches to the MAG birth question — an architecture-only inference from the ≥ 51% linker-free convergent fraction, and a protein-only miniprot survey against *Emiliania huxleyi* and two sister MAGs that left 73 / 100 sampled loci unresolved at the orthology stage — are superseded by the 682-MAG reciprocal-best-hit analysis reported above, which resolves the ancestral state at gene-model resolution across many independent orthologs per locus and yields the naive 15.4 : 1 confident birth : fill ratio on the full informative set (17.6 : 1 restricted to the ≥85%-definition telotrons; ≈3–7 : 1 after the comparator-annotation control).

### Telotron-positive lineages share a permissive cellular environment

The five Eimeria species and the haptophyte-like Tara MAG that carry telotrons are deeply divergent eukaryotes; their closest sequenced relatives that share the same telomere repeat carry none. We surveyed candidate cellular factors that could distinguish positive from negative lineages. The clearest signal is not a single cellular factor but the amount of telomerase-deposited sequence already present in the genome.

To test whether telotron-rich lineages are also enriched in interstitial telomeric sequences (ITS), we built a cap-aware ITS mask per genome using `tidk` (Brown et al. 2023, https://github.com/tolkit/tidk), the Sanger telomere identification toolkit, replacing fixed-distance positional rules that over-mask fragmented assemblies. We cross-validated tidk's cap calls against Telogator2 (Stephens & Kocher 2024, https://doi.org/10.1186/s12859-024-05803-9) on 85 cap arms of the three Eimeria T2T long-read assemblies (tenella, maxima, acervulina), finding agreement at 75 / 85 arms with median boundary difference of 44 bp (95th percentile 242 bp). Gene bodies were taken from the RefSeq GFF where available and from a miniprot CDS projection of related proteomes for the TARA MAG; Giulotto-grade ITS were then identified as ≥ 4-unit, ≥ 85.7%-identity arrays (Azzalin et al. 2001; Nergadze et al. 2007; Santagostino et al. 2020) in non-cap, non-genic sequence. Across the n = 11 Apicomplexa panel (the five telotron-positive Eimeria versus six telotron-negative close relatives Toxoplasma, Neospora, Cyclospora, Besnoitia, Cystoisospora, Cryptosporidium), Eimeria carries a median of 220 Giulotto ITS per eligible Mb versus 0.02 in the sisters (Toxo 0.12, Cyclospora 0.36, Cystoisospora 0.04; Neospora, Besnoitia, Cryptosporidium 0), a ~10⁴-fold elevation (Figure 2; Mann-Whitney one-sided P = 3.8 × 10⁻³, n = 5 vs 6). Telotron count and per-Mb ITS density covary across the eleven Apicomplexa at Spearman ρ = 0.89, P = 2.9 × 10⁻⁴. The cap-aware mask also resolves the haptophyte/stramenopile comparison: the telotron-bearing TARA stramenopile MAG carries 559 ITS per eligible Mb versus 3.8 in *Emiliania huxleyi*, consistent with its large telotron count (2,877), where an earlier positional 5 kb terminal-mask had produced an apparent inversion by erasing 99.9% of the fragmented MAG (median contig 3.4 kb) before scanning. The MAG comparison is N = 1 vs N = 1 and is reported as a direction only; the Apicomplexa Mann-Whitney remains the statistical anchor. Eimeria genomes therefore carry several thousand-fold more interstitial telomere arrays per eligible Mb than their telotron-negative sister coccidians; the same genomes carry telotrons, and the sister genomes do not.

We considered retrotransposon-derived reverse-transcriptase (RT) pool size as an alternative predictor, on the reasoning that telomerase is itself a reverse transcriptase and shared regulatory factors might restrain both. Across the same ten-species apicomplexan panel the full-length RT-ORF count gives ρ = 0.41 (p = 0.24), and survives only after excluding *Cystoisospora suis*, which carries the largest apicomplexan RT pool tested (74 full-length ORFs) but zero telotrons. The C. suis pool is a genuine active LINE-like family (single 50%-identity cluster of 63 of 87 ORFs, no internal stops) rather than annotation noise, but its raw count is inflated by assembly fragmentation: 70 of 87 ORFs sit on contigs ≤ 10 kb, against 0 of 17 in the chromosome-scale E. tenella assembly. The qualitative comparison (active RT family present in both telotron-positive Eimeria and telotron-free C. suis) is robust; the absolute count comparison is not. The relationship is also not corroborated in the Tara MAGs: TARA_PSW_86_MAG_00284 with 2,877 telotrons carries only 4 full-length RT ORFs, fewer than TARA_MED_95_MAG_00464 with 5 telotrons (6 RT ORFs). At the per-locus level the test is also negative: 0 of 280 Eimeria telotron flanks contain LTR-retrotransposon debris against an 87.5% positive-control rate on host-gene loci. Telotrons appear to be products of telomerase itself, not of the broader retrotransposon machinery.

We tested an extensive panel of candidate cellular factors that could specifically gate telomerase access to internal double-strand breaks, organised into three mechanistic categories. (i) DSB-pathway-choice factors (53BP1/TP53BP1, RIF1, CtIP/Sae2, POLQ, LIG4, XRCC4, DNA-PKcs/PRKDC, Artemis/DCLRE1C, Shieldin SHLD2, REV7/MAD2L2) and the direct telomerase-at-DSB suppressor Pif1: none distinguishes telotron-positive from telotron-negative lineages. Pif1 is the most informative test: yeast and mammalian Pif1 family helicases inhibit telomerase action at internal DSBs by 200–1000-fold. Pif1 is absent in all twelve apicomplexan genomes tested by HMM search (Pfam PF05970), consistent with a deep apicomplexan-stem loss; *Plasmodium falciparum* and *P. berghei* (apicomplexan but telotron-free) also lack it, while the haptophyte-like Tara MAG with 2,877 telotrons carries an intact 442-aa Pif1 with hmmsearch i-E = 1.2 × 10⁻³⁹, stronger than the human Pif1 hit. Pif1 loss is therefore neither sufficient (Plasmodium lacks it without telotrons) nor necessary (the largest telotron-positive lineage has it). (ii) Telomerase-RNP regulators (Dyskerin/DKC1, NOP10, GAR1, NHP2, TPP1/ACD, TCAB1/WRAP53) show the same pattern in both lineages: the core components (DKC1, WRAP53, NHP2) are present in telotron-positive and telotron-negative genomes alike, including the telotron-positive haptophyte MAG (i-E = 8.4 × 10⁻⁴⁸, 1.3 × 10⁻¹³, 7.7 × 10⁻¹⁷) and its telotron-negative MAG sisters, while TPP1, NOP10 and GAR1 are absent or below detection irrespective of telotron status. (iii) R-loop / G-quadruplex resolvers (RNase H1, RNase H2A, Senataxin/SETX, RTEL1, DHX9) are pan-eukaryotic and present in every apicomplexan tested and in the telotron-positive haptophyte MAG (SETX i-E = 1.3 × 10⁻⁴⁶, RTEL1 5.0 × 10⁻⁶⁵, DHX9 1.1 × 10⁻³⁷, RNase H1 1.5 × 10⁻⁷), telotron-positive or not. In the most controlled comparison available, the telotron-bearing haptophyte MAG is indistinguishable across the panel from its closest telotron-negative MAG relatives. Across the full 21-factor panel × 17 lineages spanning both telotron-expanding lineages (Apicomplexa and the haptophyte MAG) and their telotron-negative sisters, zero factors show a clean presence/absence split between telotron-positive lineages and their close negative sisters; the strongest |ρ| against telotron count is below 0.5. This panel is exploratory rather than a powered test: telotron expansions represent only two phylogenetically independent gains (the Eimeria stem and the haptophyte MAG), so a 21-factor screen over so few independent events cannot reach significance for any single factor after multiple-testing correction. We therefore read this result as the absence of any *obvious* single permissive correlate — not as evidence that a given factor is *not required* — and we could not test the requirement of any individual factor with the available power. Separately, to guard against selective reporting across the many hypothesis tests in this study, we applied a Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction across the eight principal confirmatory p-values reported in the manuscript (supplementary FDR table): six survive at FDR 0.05. The two that do not are both intended nulls — the DNA-repair/replication enrichment test (p = 0.56; repair genes are matched to, not enriched against, background) and the telotron-vs-control splice-fraction test (p = 0.899; telotrons splice indistinguishably from ordinary introns). The exploratory cellular-factor panel and the within-lineage *Eimeria* correlations (ρ = 0.24, ρ = 0.18, and the five-species survival correlation) are excluded from this count and reported, as above, as descriptive rather than confirmatory, precisely because their unit of replication is a single lineage.

Two non-target probes confirm there is no genome-scale signal we are missing. (i) Twelve telomere-protection and DSB-repair factors from an independent earlier sweep (ATR, Cdc13/CTC1, Ku70, Ku80, Mre11, Nbs1, Pot1, Rad50, Rap1, Stn1, Ten1, plus Pif1 family extras) showed no Eimeria-specific losses. (ii) G-quadruplex propensity ratio of telotron-positive vs sister apicomplexans collapses after masking all rotations and reverse complements of TTAGGG / TTTAGGG / TTAGG from the input: the within-apicomplexa Spearman correlation between non-telomeric G4 density and telotron count falls from ρ = 0.51 (p = 0.16, n = 9) unmasked to ρ = 0.05 (p = 0.89) after masking, and the Eimeria/Toxoplasma G4 density ratio falls from 1.33× unmasked to 1.00× under permissive masking. The unmasked apparent association was the telomere-repeat motifs themselves tripping the G4 caller; no non-telomeric G4 signal survives. Spatial autocorrelation of telotrons in Eimeria is also not elevated relative to sisters (Moran's I = -0.08 to -0.04 in Eimeria vs +0.00 to +0.03 in sisters), arguing against an AT-rich-domain DSB-supply mechanism.

The pattern that emerges is simpler than a permissive-cellular-factor model: the telotron-positive lineages are those whose genomes already record extensive telomerase deposition at every measurable location (terminal telomere arrays, interstitial telomere arrays, and intronic telotrons). Telotrons are the subset of those deposition events that landed at a position whose host context permits splicing.


### Conclusion

Telomerase action at internal double-strand breaks is a recurrent, large-scale source of new spliceosomal introns. In the haptophyte-like Tara MAG the process operates predominantly as *de novo* intron birth, creating thousands of new introns at genomic positions that were intron-free in the ancestor (confident birth : fill ≈ 3–7 : 1 under a strict comparator-annotation control, ~15–18 : 1 naive, among resolvable loci); in *Eimeria* the same reaction instead overwrites the interiors of pre-existing ancestral introns in place, leaving their splice boundaries intact. These are the two limiting outcomes of one mechanism, determined by whether the healed break falls in a previously intron-free site or inside an existing intron. The mechanism operates in two confirmed eukaryotic lineages spanning the Haptophyta and Apicomplexa, generates dozens to thousands of new introns per permissive genome, and produces splice-compatible introns through several architecturally distinct routes that vary by lineage. A light scan of all 683 Tara Oceans eukaryotic MAGs indicates the flagship is not isolated: 19 MAGs carry ≥1,000 introns bearing a telomeric-repeat array and 13 carry ≥100 convergent (mixed G-rich + C-rich) intronic arrays — the cleanest telotron signature — led by TARA_PON_109_MAG_00250 (9,580 array introns, 1,713 convergent) and TARA_AOS_82_MAG_00154 (8,369 / 1,485). These are candidate expansions from an unvalidated scan (no dedup, no ancestral-state resolution, no per-locus splice confirmation) and we report them as leads warranting dedicated study rather than as confirmed telotron lineages; the point is that the flagship's telotron burst has parallels across marine protist MAGs and the phenomenon is unlikely to be a single-genome peculiarity. The boundary signatures of each architecture preserve a molecular fossil of the deposition event filtered through spliceosomal compatibility: the array endpoint sits at the spliceosomal cut, the rotation register reports the splice-compatible subset of deposition events, and convergent two-arm telotrons retain a geometric record of bidirectional telomerase extension from a single break.

The mechanism we describe explains how telotrons arise; why they persist in a lineage is a separate question, shaped by the population-genetic context that determines the fate of slightly deleterious insertions (Lynch and Conery 2003, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1089370) and by the species-level amount of telomerase product already present at every observable position in the genome (see the permissive-cellular-environment analysis above). The catalog of cellular machines capable of producing new gene structures at this scale therefore contains at least two distinct entries, transposable elements for introner-mediated gains and telomerase for telotron-mediated gains, and the search for further mechanisms remains open.

Telomerase and the spliceosome are among the eukaryotic-specific features inferred to have been present in the last eukaryotic common ancestor (Koonin 2006, https://doi.org/10.1186/1745-6150-1-22). Their joint presence in every modern eukaryotic lineage means that the capacity for telomerase-templated intron generation has been intrinsically available since the eukaryotic stem, whether or not it has been exercised at telotron-burst scale in any particular lineage. The two systems we observe expanding telotrons today (Haptophyta, Apicomplexa) and the candidate stramenopile signal are recent realisations of an ancient potential rather than evidence of a coupling unique to those lineages.
